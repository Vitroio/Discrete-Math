One final definition: We say a graph is bipartite if the vertices can be divided into two sets, \(A\) and \(B\text{,}\) with no two vertices in \(A\) adjacent and no two vertices in \(B\) adjacent. The vertices in \(A\) can be adjacent to some or all of the vertices in \(B\text{.}\) If each vertex in \(A\) is adjacent to all the vertices in \(B\text{,}\) then the graph is a complete bipartite graph, and gets a special name: \(K_{m,n}\text{,}\) where \(|A| = m\) and \(|B| = n\text{.}\)
